Serious about the basics.

Four stages, one standard. The syllabus follows the CBSE curriculum. The teaching is planned around how children actually learn: in order, with practice, and with someone paying attention.

The stages

From first stories to board exams.

Each stage has its own demands. You can read what we focus on, and what a child should be able to do by the end of it.

Early Years Nursery to UKG

The early years are for language, number, movement and the habits of being in a group. Work is playful but deliberate: stories, rhyme, counting, drawing, sand, water, blocks and a great deal of talk.

  • Daily story and rhyme time
  • Phonics, pre-writing and early writing
  • Number sense through play and materials
  • Art, music and outdoor movement
  • Snack, rest and social routines
Primary Grades 1 to 5

The primary years build fluency. Children read longer texts, write with structure and learn to explain mathematical thinking rather than guess. Subjects stay connected, so a lesson in one class is useful in another.

  • Guided and independent reading
  • Grammar, spelling and clear composition
  • Number operations with understanding
  • Environmental studies and observation
  • Art, music, sport and library time
Middle School Grades 6 to 8

The middle years introduce specialised subject teaching and more independent work. Children learn to manage a timetable, take notes, revise and ask a question that has more than one answer.

  • Subject teachers and laboratory work
  • Note-making, research and presentation
  • Algebra, geometry and scientific method
  • Second and third language development
  • Projects, sport and creative electives
Senior School Grades 9 and 10

Two years of board preparation, without turning school into a coaching centre. The work gets harder. The support gets more precise. Revision is taught, not assumed.

  • CBSE syllabus and examination practice
  • Structured revision and paper technique
  • Practical work in science and maths
  • Subject clinics for specific gaps
  • Time management and exam wellbeing

The senior years

Preparation, without the panic.

Board exams reward steady work more than last-minute intensity. From the start of Grade 9, students work to a calendar: what is being taught, when it will be revised, and how to use a practice paper properly.

Teachers review common errors across the class and give individual feedback where it matters. The aim is a student who can sit an unfamiliar question, think, and begin.

Assessment

Assessment should explain, not rank.

We use a mix of classwork, periodic tests and term examinations. The point is to find the next teaching step, not to sort children into winners and losers. Parents receive specifics, not a vague average.

  • Regular reading and number checks in the junior years
  • Written feedback on major pieces of work
  • Parent meetings with concrete next steps
  • A support plan when progress stalls

Learning support

No child quietly left behind.

Some children need more time, more practice or a different explanation. That is normal, and it is easier to address early. Teachers flag a concern, the coordinator looks at the work, and a short, specific plan follows.

Support can mean a small group, a different resource or a conversation with the family. It is reviewed, not left to run indefinitely.
