Built for the whole day.

A school is not a corridor of classrooms. It is a library, a ground, a place to make things, a place to sit quietly and a hundred small routines that tell a child they belong here.

Facilities

What the building makes possible.

Facilities matter when they are used. These are the spaces we put to work every week.

Library and reading room

A quiet place to choose a book, read and borrow. Reading time is timetabled, not squeezed in.

Science and mathematics

Practical work and equipment that let children test an idea instead of only reading about it.

Digital learning

Screens used where they add something: research, practice, coding and a clearer explanation.

Sport and outdoor play

A ground for team games, space to run and a daily break that belongs to the children.

Art and music

Materials, instruments and room to draw, make, sing and perform without apologising for the noise.

Care and first aid

A first-aid point, clean drinking water and adults who know each child's routine and allergies.

The year

The year has a rhythm.

Children remember the repetition: the Monday assembly, the reading hour, the match after school, the annual day they practised for weeks. A good school year is patterned, not packed.

Alongside the timetable, the calendar holds the things that make a school a community. Celebrations, exhibitions, sport and service give every child a second and third way to contribute.

A normal day

Structure first. Then the freedom to work.

Days begin with assembly and settle into lessons. Breaks are real breaks. Sport, art and library time appear on the timetable rather than in a promise.

  • Assembly and notices to start the day
  • Morning lessons when attention is strongest
  • Break, lunch and outdoor time
  • Afternoon lessons, practice and activities
  • A calm, supervised dismissal
